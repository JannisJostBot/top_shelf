HCover + rink (between E and F)

F's cover on top, E's rink below. The program opens with the game's own animated title scene, printed with the halftone screen, and under it the rink diagram is the menu: Play on the centre face-off circle, Train and Watch in one end zone, Teams and Arena in the other, the line-up on the players' bench, Setup in the penalty box, Keys and Guide by the officials. The matchup prints on the ticket stub that tears off to play. On a desktop the cover, matchup and ticket stand on the left and the rink lies sideways on the right.

  • The cover says "Top Shelf, a hockey game" in the game's art; the rink makes finding your way around feel like the game.
  • Fewer words on screen than F: the rink's spots are the labels.
  • Ideas to refine: the puck could slide to the circle you pick before it opens; the selected circle fills with ink.

EThe rink is the menu

The page is a rink diagram printed on ice-white stock: boards, red centre line, blue lines, face-off circles, creases and nets, exactly as you see the ice in the game's top-down view. Every section sits on the spot of the rink where it belongs: Play on the centre face-off circle with the puck on the dot, Train and Watch in the end-zone circles, Line-up on the players' bench, Setup in the penalty box, Teams and Arena in the other zone, Keys and Guide at the referee's crease. The blue and red lines are the two inks, so each arena prints its own rink.

  • Unmistakably hockey; it is the same picture as the game, so moving from menu to match feels continuous.
  • Picking a circle could "drop the puck" there: a small animation before that section opens.
  • Phone shows the rink upright like in the game; desktop turns it sideways with the ticket beside it.

FGame-art cover

The program's cover is the game itself: the animated title scene from Top Shelf (the crowd, the boards, a skater winding up and burying a slap shot top shelf) reprinted with a halftone screen and the second ink a hair off register, like a riso print. Below it the matchup prints as a scoreboard in team colours, the sections are a contents list with pucks as bullets, and the ticket stub tears off to play. (The cover here runs the game's real title art.)

  • The hockey is the first thing you see, in the game's own pixel art, so menu and match are one product.
  • Keeps everything you liked about D: paper, two inks per arena, contents list, ticket stub.
  • The cover animates (the shot, the crowd jumping), the rest stays calm and readable.

GNight edition scoresheet

The program printed on dark stock, the same deep navy as the game's screens, with ice-white and arena inks. The menu is the official game sheet a scorekeeper fills in: the header holds the arena, CPU level and periods (tap to change), the roster table is your line-up (numbers, names, positions circled like on a real sheet, ratings as tally marks), and "Drop the puck" is the referee's stamp. The other sections are a contents strip.

  • Keeps the game's dark look, so it clearly belongs to the same product; hockey through its paperwork.
  • The line-up and match settings are visible right on the first screen, no extra tabs.
  • Most information on one page; phone layout needs the most care.
