All four keep the same contents (Play, Watch, Train, Teams and line-up, Arena, Setup, Keys, Guide) and the "Made with AI" note, and each is shown on a phone and on a desktop screen. ACoin-op cabinet

The menu is an arcade machine. A lit marquee with chasing bulbs carries the title, the live 3D game plays as attract mode on a curved CRT, and the control panel below has real arcade buttons: one big red PLAY, WATCH and TRAIN beside it. Setup and the other sections are a row of labelled switches. On desktop the cabinet stands in a dim arcade with side art showing your record and the arena.

  • Strongest "game" feel, very readable on phones (big round targets for thumbs).
  • The pixel logo, LED HUD and crowd art all fit an arcade era.
  • Risk: arcade cabinets are a known trope; craft is in the details (wood grain, coin slot, bulb chase).

BCoach's whiteboard

The menu is the whiteboard in the locker room. The title and menu are written in marker on a rink-shaped board, the selected item is circled and underlined, your skaters are coloured magnets (the line-up manager lives right here: drag the magnets onto C, W and D), a sticky note holds the match settings and the arena is a ticket stub taped on. On desktop the board hangs beside your jersey on a hook.

  • The most hand-made, warm direction; turns the line-up into the menu's centrepiece.
  • Mixes a marker typeface with the pixel fonts: needs care so the in-game pixel look still connects.
  • Risk: busier; small text on a phone needs testing.

CJumbotron broadcast

The menu lives inside the arena. The live 3D demo fills the screen, the title is on the centre-hung jumbotron in the same dot-matrix LEDs as the in-game scoreboard, the sections run along the ribbon board that circles the stands (the selected one lit), and tonight's match is a TV lower-third in the team colours, with a LIVE bug. Every panel (teams, setup...) slides up as a broadcast graphic.

  • Most consistent with what is already in the game (LED HUD, 3D arena, broadcast cameras).
  • The menu changes look with every arena automatically: its colours, roof and crowd are the backdrop.
  • Risk: closest to today's layout in spirit; needs strong motion (ribbon scroll, wipe-ins) to feel crafted.

DMatchday program

The menu is a printed matchday program, risograph style: two ink colours taken from the selected arena (blue and red for Frost Hollow, pink and cyan for Neon...), halftone dots, slightly misaligned ink layers and paper grain. The cover shows a halftone skater and the title, the sections are the table of contents, and the match is a ticket stub with a perforated edge to tear off (Play).

  • The most distinctive, editorial and "indie" look; a bold contrast to the dark game screens.
  • Every arena reprints the program in its own inks.
  • Risk: a light, paper menu in front of a dark game; the cut into a match needs a good transition.
