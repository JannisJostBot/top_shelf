    09 · Rulebook

    Guide

    The basics

    Three skaters and a goalie a side. Most goals after three periods wins; a tie goes to overtime, where the next goal wins. You control the skater with the marker: you automatically take over whoever has the puck or is receiving your pass, otherwise the skater nearest the puck. Switch jumps to the nearest one yourself.

    A match opens with a short fly-through of the arena; any button skips it. Pause any time (P, Esc or the II button); the game also pauses when you switch to another tab.

    Skating

    Carrying the puck slows you down by a tenth. Pokes and dashes give a short burst of speed. Stopping hard throws up a spray of ice. From a standstill skaters take a few short, quick strides, then long ones that push out to the side; easing off, they glide on both skates.

    Shooting

    Hold Shoot to wind up (up to about 0.7 s), release to fire. A full wind-up shoots almost twice as hard but less accurately. In the attacking zone, facing the net, the shot goes for a corner: steer left or right to pick one, or let it pick the side the goalie leaves open. With the mouse, the aim arrow picks the spot. Anywhere else you shoot straight ahead, which is handy for dumping the puck in.

    Passing

    The pass goes to the teammate in the direction you are steering. With no direction it finds the most open teammate up the ice. While a pass is on its way to you, your skater leans toward it, but steering hard away can still lose it.

    Defence

    • Pokelunges at the carrier. It only works from the front or side and at close range; from behind you get NO REACH. Good hands (HND) against their hands raise the odds. A miss leaves you stunned for a moment. Staying close also strips the puck now and then.
    • Checkcharges into a nearby carrier. Your checking (CHK) against theirs, plus how fast you arrive, decides it: they go down and lose the puck, they stumble, or you bounce off.
    • Dashis what Check does when nobody has the puck: a burst toward it.

    Face-offs

    Wait for DROP!, then press Shoot. You have to beat the CPU's reaction: 0.42 s on Rookie, 0.31 s on Pro, 0.24 s on All-Star. Pressing too early hands them the draw.

    Periods, overtime and a frozen puck

    Three periods of 0:30 to 5:00 each (Match page). A tie after the third goes to overtime with no clock (SD on the scoreboard): the next goal wins. A loose puck nobody touches for five seconds is frozen and faced off again at centre ice.

    Goalies

    Goalies react to the shot and can catch and hold it. Hard shots and the far corner beat them most often; shots off the post ring away. A catch goes in the glove, a deflection off the blocker, and a pad save is made in the butterfly, down on the knees with the pads flat on the ice.

    Player ratings

    SPD skating speed, SHT shot power, HND puck handling (stealing and keeping the puck), CHK checking. Wingers shoot harder and skate faster, centers have the best hands, defensemen check hardest but are slower. Every match rolls a fresh roster, unless you set your line-up first (the Line-up bench on the left of the rink): it shows your players' ratings and lets you pick who plays centre, wing and defence. The centre takes the face-offs.

    Momentum

    Winning a fight (25 s) or polishing at least half of the ice between periods (20 s) makes your team skate 6% faster; a bolt and the seconds left show next to the team's score. Online, the resurfacing boost goes to whoever polished the bigger share of their half.

    Ice wear and the resurfacer

    Skates chew up the ice as the period goes on, and worn ice drags on the puck so passes and shots slow down sooner. Between periods you drive the resurfacer for 30 seconds: steer with the stick and cover as much ice as you can (half earns the speed boost). Shoot sounds the horn, Pass finishes early. The boards guide the machine along instead of stopping it, and it fits behind the nets. Clean ice plays fast again. Turn the resurfacing off in Setup.

    Fights

    After you score, a sore loser may drop the gloves: at most once a match, more likely with every goal until it happens (turn it off in Setup). Punch to hit; Block when they wind up and the red ! shows. Punch late in their wind-up to interrupt it and land a big hit; a punch into their guard gets blocked and may be countered. Punching drains stamina, so tired punches are weak; it recovers when you wait. Higher CPU levels wind up faster, block and counter more. The winner's team skates faster for 25 s; after 22 s without a knockout the refs break it up. Online, a fight can follow either side's goal (the host's Fights setting, once a match) and you face the other player: every punch winds up with the red ! first, so a block in time stops it; a blocked or interrupted fighter is left open.

    CPU level and style

    The level raises the CPU's skating speed, stealing, goalie skill, shooting accuracy, reactions, face-off speed, checking and how often its carriers dodge a check, and makes the puck harder for you to steal. The style (on the Match page) changes how skaters play: Classic is the original arcade AI; Realistic skaters react with a short delay, skate in arcs, keep their positions, mark a man on defence, deke and wind up before they shoot.

    CPU defenders are not perfect: they read a sharp cut a moment late, poke in separate tries (harder against a carrier at speed), and back off to reset when they cannot win the puck. Keep your feet moving. A carrier who just stands still gets pressed, and then double-teamed.

    Two goals behind, the CPU changes its tactics: it presses sooner and longer, shoots from further out and sends its defenceman forward, and plays a touch faster and harder. Three or more behind, twice as much. Your own CPU teammates never do this.

    Celebrations and replays

    After a goal the scorers celebrate (turn it off in Setup), then the goal is replayed from changing camera angles, some of them in 3D. The camera button, V or Y on a controller shows the next angle; the skip button, Enter, View, a click or a tap skips ahead.

    Watching

    Watch CPU vs CPU plays a match by itself, filmed like a broadcast: Cinematic cuts between the top-down view and 3D shots from the ice, the nets and the stands; Classic keeps the top-down view. Switch with the camera button in the bottom right corner, C, or X on a controller; V or Y cuts to the next shot.

    Online

    Host a room on the Online page and send your friend the code, or join with theirs. The host's browser runs the match with the host's settings; the guest sees it from their own end, so both attack up the screen, and each of you steers one skater with CPU teammates. Face-offs are timed on each screen from the moment the puck drops there, so the connection does not slow anyone down; faster than 0.1 s counts as jumping the draw. Between periods you both resurface your own half (20 s, a slower machine) and the bigger share wins the boost. Each side checks what the other reports (face-off times, goals, resurfacing) and notes anything that does not add up. Fights are between the two of you. Your own skater moves the moment you steer, on your screen; the rest of the match reaches the guest a moment later. Either player can pause or resume, a rematch needs both of you, and leaving ends the match for both. The invite code works for one guest, once.

    Arenas and looks

    Six arenas, each with its own home team, ice, lines, roof and goal song. Jerseys, sticks and pucks (Teams page) are looks only: they never change how anyone plays. The camera (Setup) follows the play close up, shows the rink from board to board, or adds the stands.

    Training

    The Train tab has free skate (pick opponents, teammates and their goalie), the aim trainer (a board covers the goal except a small gap that moves after every goal; one minute or endless, with a still or a sliding gap), fight practice and resurfacer practice.
