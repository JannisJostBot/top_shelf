    09 · Rulebook

    Guide

    The basics

    Three skaters and a goalie a side. Most goals after three periods wins; a tie goes to overtime, where the next goal wins. You control the skater with the marker: you automatically take over whoever has the puck or is receiving your pass, otherwise the skater nearest the puck. Switch jumps to the nearest one yourself.

    A match opens with a short fly-through of the arena; any button skips it. Pause any time (P, Esc or the II button); the game also pauses when you switch to another tab. The pause menu switches the sound on and off, as does the Sound row under Setup.

    On a phone or tablet, add the game to your home screen: it then opens fullscreen like an app, without the browser's bars. The first time you open it in a mobile browser a tip shows how (tick "Don't show again" to keep it away).

    Skating

    Carrying the puck slows you down by a tenth. Pokes and dashes give a short burst of speed. Stopping hard throws up a fan of ice spray, for every skater. From a standstill skaters take a few short, quick strides, then long ones that push out to the side; easing off, they glide on both skates.

    Shooting

    Hold Shoot to wind up (up to about 0.7 s), release to fire. A full wind-up shoots almost twice as hard but less accurately. In the attacking zone, facing the net, the shot goes for a corner: steer left or right to pick one, or let it pick the side the goalie leaves open. With the mouse, the aim arrow picks the spot. Anywhere else you shoot straight ahead, which is handy for dumping the puck in.

    Passing

    The pass goes to the teammate in the direction you are steering. With no direction it finds the most open teammate up the ice. While a pass is on its way to you, your skater leans toward it, but steering hard away can still lose it.

    Defence

    • Pokelunges at the carrier. It only works from the front or side and at close range; from behind you get NO REACH. Good hands (HND) against their hands raise the odds. A miss leaves you stunned for a moment. Staying close also strips the puck now and then.
    • Checkcharges into a nearby carrier. Your checking (CHK) against theirs, plus how fast you arrive, decides it: they go down and lose the puck, they stumble, or you bounce off.
    • Dashis what Check does when nobody has the puck: a burst toward it.

    Face-offs

    Wait for DROP!, then press Shoot. You have to beat the CPU's reaction: 0.42 s on Rookie, 0.31 s on Pro, 0.24 s on All-Star. Pressing too early hands them the draw.

    Periods, overtime and a frozen puck

    Three periods of 0:30 to 5:00 each (Match page). A tie after the third goes to overtime with no clock (SD on the scoreboard): the next goal wins. A loose puck nobody touches for five seconds is frozen and faced off again at centre ice.

    Goalies

    Goalies react to the shot and can catch and hold it. Hard shots and the far corner beat them most often; shots off the post ring away. A catch goes in the glove, a deflection off the blocker, and a pad save is made in the butterfly, down on the knees with the pads flat on the ice.

    Player ratings

    SPD skating speed, SHT shot power, HND puck handling (stealing and keeping the puck), CHK checking. Wingers shoot harder and skate faster, centers have the best hands, defensemen check hardest but are slower. Every match rolls a fresh roster, unless you set your line-up first (the Line-up spot at the top right of the rink): it draws your players at their positions on a small rink, shows their ratings and lets you pick who plays centre, wing and defence (tap two skaters on the rink to swap them). The centre takes the face-offs.

    Momentum

    Winning a fight (25 s) or polishing at least half of the ice between periods (20 s) makes your team skate 6% faster; a bolt and the seconds left show next to the team's score. Online, the resurfacing boost goes to whoever polished the bigger share of their half.

    Ice wear and the resurfacer

    Skates chew up the ice as the period goes on, and worn ice drags on the puck so passes and shots slow down sooner. Between periods you drive the resurfacer for 30 seconds: steer with the stick and cover as much ice as you can (half earns the speed boost). Shoot sounds the horn, Pass finishes early. The boards guide the machine along instead of stopping it, and it fits behind the nets. Clean ice plays fast again. Turn the resurfacing off in Setup.

    Fights

    After you score, a sore loser may drop the gloves: at most once a match, more likely with every goal until it happens (turn it off in Setup). Punch to hit; Block when they wind up and the red ! shows. Punch late in their wind-up to interrupt it and land a big hit; a punch into their guard gets blocked and may be countered. Punching drains stamina, so tired punches are weak; it recovers when you wait. Higher CPU levels wind up faster, block and counter more. The winner's team skates faster for 25 s; after 22 s without a knockout the refs break it up.

    Online, nobody drops the gloves by chance: during every goal replay each of you can vote to fight (the FIGHT? button, G, or LT). It only happens if you both vote; the button blinks when the other player wants it, and you can take your vote back. Once you both have, the replay stops and you face each other; after the fight the game goes on with the face-off. One fight a match, so after it the vote is gone (the host's Fights setting turns it off). Every punch winds up with the red ! first, so a block in time stops it; a blocked or interrupted fighter is left open.

    CPU level and style

    The level raises the CPU's skating speed, stealing, goalie skill, shooting accuracy, reactions, face-off speed, checking and how often its carriers dodge a check, and makes the puck harder for you to steal. The style (on the Match page) changes how skaters play: Classic is the original arcade AI; Realistic skaters react with a short delay, skate in arcs, keep their positions, mark a man on defence, deke and wind up before they shoot.

    CPU defenders are not perfect: they read a sharp cut a moment late, poke in separate tries (harder against a carrier at speed), and back off to reset when they cannot win the puck. Keep your feet moving. A carrier who just stands still gets pressed, and then double-teamed.

    Two goals behind, the CPU changes its tactics: it presses sooner and longer, shoots from further out and sends its defenceman forward, and plays a touch faster and harder. Three or more behind, twice as much. Your own CPU teammates never do this.

    Celebrations and replays

    After a goal the scorers celebrate (turn it off in Setup), then the goal is replayed from changing camera angles, some of them in 3D. The camera button, V or Y on a controller shows the next angle; the skip button, Enter, View, a click or a tap skips ahead.

    Online, the replay is also the time to talk back: six pixel emotes (hyped, nice, LOL, what?!, nooo, grrr): tap the face button at the bottom to open them and pick one (a tap anywhere else closes them), or use keys 1 to 6, or the D-pad, L3 and R3 on a controller. Yours pops up on the left, the other player's on the right, one about every second at most.

    Watching

    Watch CPU vs CPU (the Watch bench on the left of the rink) plays a match by itself, filmed like a broadcast: Cinematic cuts between the top-down view and 3D shots from the ice, the nets and the stands; Classic keeps the top-down view. Switch with the camera button in the bottom right corner, C, or X on a controller; V or Y cuts to the next shot.

    Online

    Host a room on the Online page and send your friend the code, the link (it opens the game with the code filled in; the code is scrambled in it, so nobody can read it off the link) or the QR code to scan with a phone, or join with theirs; once you are both in, the host drops the puck with the ticket at the bottom of the page. The host's browser runs the match with the host's settings; the guest sees it from their own end, so both attack up the screen, and each of you steers one skater with CPU teammates. Face-offs are timed on each screen from the moment the puck drops there, so the connection does not slow anyone down; faster than 0.1 s counts as jumping the draw. Between periods you both resurface your own half (20 s, a slower machine) and the bigger share wins the boost. Each side checks what the other reports (face-off times, goals, resurfacing) and notes anything that does not add up. Fights are between the two of you, and only when you both vote for one during a replay; replays also let you send emotes (see above). Your own skater moves the moment you steer, on your screen; the rest of the match reaches the guest a moment later: 0.07 s behind on a steady connection, up to a quarter of a second on a patchy one (it adjusts by itself). Either player can pause or resume, a rematch needs both of you, and leaving ends the match for both. The invite code works for one guest, once.

    Arenas and looks

    Six arenas, each with its own home team, ice, lines, roof and goal song. Jerseys, sticks and pucks (Teams page) are looks only: they never change how anyone plays. The camera (Setup) follows the play close up, shows the rink from board to board, or adds the stands.

    Overdrive graphics

    Overdrive (Setup, off by default) adds effects that cost more to draw, so it suits a fast computer more than a phone. It only changes the picture: the match plays the same, and online each player picks it for their own screen.

    The arena is lit for real: each arena's lamps light the ice in their own colour, a little dimmer between them, the boards and corners fall into shadow and the stands go dark, a soft follow spot stays on the puck (on the scorer after a goal, on the fighters in a fight). The players are lit and shaded by the same light as the ice; only your marker stays bright, with a faint glow. The goal light, flares, sparks and camera flashes light up what is around them.

    On the ice: players and the puck are mirrored in the ice, the reflections drawn at your screen's full resolution, soft and wavering a little, broken up by fine grooves and scratches in the ice and fading below the feet, with soft shadows; skates leave scratches and the spray from hard stops settles as snow that piles against the boards, until the resurfacer wipes both away. Hard shots leave a hot trail and a shockwave, a puck off the post throws sparks, a lofted puck casts a shadow, fast skaters trail speed lines and a boosted team leaves afterimages. Players breathe clouds at face-offs and stoppages; in a fight the gloves and stick spin away and a knockout punch sends the helmet flying.

    Around it: a goal sets off a light show (the lamps chase in the scorer's colour), the spinning goal light, confetti and streamers from the stands, flares and a burst of camera flashes; the scoreboard flashes GOAL! and shows REPLAY during replays, and the crowd holds up cards when it gets loud. A big hit freezes the picture for a split second (three frames), with a white flash and a short colour split; a goal gets the colour split too. Goal replays ease into slow motion around the shot (not online, where both screens play the replay together) and blur the edges of the frame. The 3D views add haze and light beams from the roof, adverts on the ribbon board and, behind the menu, finer pixels. With reduced motion set on your device the freeze, the white flash and the colour split stay off.

    Training

    The Train tab has free skate (pick opponents, teammates and their goalie), the aim trainer (a board covers the goal except a small gap that moves after every goal; one minute or endless, with a still or a sliding gap), fight practice and resurfacer practice. On a wide screen the left panel keeps the drill's own numbers (goals, shots, accuracy, time, your best) in place of the match stats. Training is not a game night: only a handful of fans (about one seat in twenty) turn up to watch.
